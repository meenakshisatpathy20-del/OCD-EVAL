First assessment, Ms R (27), administrator, self-referred. C = clinician, P = patient.

P: My mum says I'm just very devout. Maybe she's right and I'm just a bad Catholic.
C: What's been happening?
P: I get thoughts. About God. Horrible things, insults, words I'd never say. Pictures I can't describe to you. About Our Lady. [long pause] It's the opposite of what I believe.
C: How much of the day?
P: About two hours, all together.
C: What do you do when they come?
P: I fight every single one. I try to push it out. It almost never works.
C: How upsetting is it?
P: Very. It's the worst part of my life.
C: Is there anything you do after a thought, to put it right?
P: I say a Hail Mary in my head. Seven times. Exactly seven. If I lose count I start again. Nobody knows, it's all in my head.
C: Anything else?
P: I go back over the thought. Did I mean it? Did I want to think it? Was there a part of me that enjoyed it? I go round and round on that.
C: How long does the praying and going back over take?
P: An hour and a half a day, maybe.
C: Do you try not to do the prayers?
P: No, I just do them. I wish I didn't need them. Sometimes I manage to skip one.
C: And if you're interrupted?
P: I just finish later. It bothers me a little.
C: Anything you've stopped doing?
P: Mass. I haven't been since Easter. It's worst in church. That's where I used to feel most at home.
C: Do you talk to anyone about it?
P: The priest. Every week, at confession. I ask him if I've sinned. I ask him again. He's very patient.
C: How's work?
P: I'm slower. I'm managing.
C: Mood? Any thoughts of harming yourself or anyone else?
P: A bit low. No, nothing like that.
