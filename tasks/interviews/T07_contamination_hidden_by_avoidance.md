First assessment, Mr S (36), referred by GP. C = clinician, P = patient.

P: Honestly, I think my GP's overreacting. It's not that bad any more. Last year I was washing four hours a day. Now it's forty minutes.
C: What were you worried about, with the washing?
P: HIV. Hepatitis. Blood on door handles, bus rails, shop counters. I know it's irrational. The risk is tiny. The thoughts still come.
C: How much of your day do they take now?
P: An hour or two? Mostly when I'm planning how to not touch things.
C: And if you think you might have touched something?
P: I panic. It's horrible. Really disturbing.
C: Do you try to push the thoughts away?
P: I make some effort. It rarely works.
C: How's your life day to day at the moment?
P: I left the buses eight months ago. I was a driver. I don't really go out now. Shopping gets delivered. My partner opens the post. I don't use public toilets, so I don't go anywhere more than half an hour from home. Which is basically nowhere.
C: And the washing, the forty minutes?
P: I just do it. I don't fight it, I wish I didn't have to. I can put it off a few minutes, if I really try. It's hard.
C: Anything else you do?
P: I ask my partner if I could have caught something. A few times a day.
C: What happens if you can't wash?
P: Last month my partner put the shopping bags on the sofa without wiping them. I had a panic attack. Didn't sleep that night.
C: Mood?
P: Low. Because of the job. I'm not going to do anything stupid, if that's what you're asking.
C: Any other unusual experiences, voices, anything like that?
P: No.
C: Alcohol?
P: Hardly ever.
