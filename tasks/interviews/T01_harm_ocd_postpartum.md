First assessment, Ms A (29), four months postpartum. Husband present for the last part. C = clinician, P = patient, H = husband.

C: What's brought you in today?
P: My health visitor made me come. I keep getting these... pictures. Of me hurting him. Stabbing him with the big knife from the kitchen. Or holding him under in the bath. [crying] I'd never. I'd rather die than hurt him.
C: When you say you'd rather die, have you had any thoughts of ending your life?
P: No. No, it's just something you say. I want to be here for him.
C: Tell me about the pictures. How often do they come?
P: When it started, about six weeks after he was born, it was all day, every day. I couldn't do anything. It's a bit less now. This past week they come out of nowhere, twenty, thirty times a day. If I added it all up, a couple of hours a day.
C: What do you do when one comes?
P: I fight it. Every single time. I try to think of something else, anything. It hardly ever works. It's back in seconds.
C: How upsetting is it?
P: It's the worst thing I've ever felt. I cry most days. I keep thinking maybe I'm dangerous. Maybe someone should take him off me.
C: Has anything changed in what you do day to day?
P: The knives are locked in the boot of the car. I don't bath him any more, he does [nods at husband], every bath. My mum moved in last month because I won't be on my own with him. I was meant to go back to work in September and I've put it off.
C: Is there anything you do to make yourself feel better when the pictures come?
P: I ask them. "Do you think I could ever hurt him?" Ten, fifteen times a day. I try not to, most of the time, but I usually end up asking anyway. Some days I can hold it in, some days I can't. And at night I lie there going back over the whole day, minute by minute, making sure I didn't do anything to him. Half an hour, forty-five minutes. I've been reading stories online about mothers who did hurt their babies too, to see if I'm like them.
C: Roughly how much time does all of that take, the asking, the going back over, the reading?
P: An hour and a half a day? This week, anyway.
C: What happens if you can't ask?
P: Last week he said he wasn't going to answer any more. I was really, really anxious. For about an hour. Then it settled.
C [to husband]: How are things at home?
H: Tiring. The questions wear me down, honestly. But we're managing. The house runs, the baby's fine. She's lovely with him when we're around.
