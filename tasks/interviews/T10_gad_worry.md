First assessment, Mrs K (45), school administrator, referred by GP. C = clinician, P = patient.

P: I think I might have OCD. My thoughts are obsessive. They go round and round and I can't switch them off.
C: What are the thoughts about?
P: Everything. The mortgage, whether we can pay it. My son's exams. My mum's heart. Whether my job's safe, there's talk of cuts. Being late. One finishes and the next one starts.
C: How long has it been like this?
P: Two and a half years. Most days.
C: How do you see the thoughts? Do they make sense to you?
P: They're real things. Bills are real. But I worry far too much about them, I know that.
C: Do you ever get thoughts or pictures that feel strange to you, or wrong, or against who you are?
P: No. It's just the worrying.
C: Is there anything you do to stop the worry, or to stop something bad happening? Anything repeated, out loud or in your head?
P: Not really. I look at the bank app maybe a couple of times a day, just to know where we are. That's normal, isn't it?
C: How's your body?
P: My neck and shoulders are like concrete. I don't sleep well. I'm snappy. Tired all the time.
C: Is there anywhere you avoid?
P: No.
C: Work, home?
P: I keep going. I look after everyone. I'm just always on edge.
C: Any panic attacks? Alcohol?
P: No. A glass of wine at the weekend.
C: Any thoughts of harming yourself or anyone else?
P: No.
