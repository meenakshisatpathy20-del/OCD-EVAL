First assessment, Mr G (52), engineering manager. Wife present. C = clinician, P = patient, W = wife.

W: He's so OCD about everything. Always has been.
P: [laughs] I suppose I've got a bit of OCD. Is that what this is?
C: Tell me what happens.
W: He redoes his team's reports. Himself. Late at night. They've missed two deadlines this year because he wouldn't sign them off until every heading was right.
P: They were sloppy. If I don't do it properly, nobody will.
C: If you let a report go out the way it was, what would happen?
P: Nothing would happen. It'd just be a worse report with my name on it. I'd be annoyed.
C: Do you feel anxious, like you have to fix it?
P: No. I like it done properly. It's satisfying, when it's right.
W: Weekends are planned on lists. Times for everything. If the kids change the plan, he's in a mood all day.
C: Anything else at home?
W: The garage. Thirty years of manuals, broken kettles, a toaster from 1998.
P: They might be useful. And we're not throwing money around. There's no need.
W: We're comfortable. There's no need for him to be like that.
C: Do you ever get thoughts that pop into your head that you don't want, that feel wrong or upsetting?
P: Not really. I think about work a lot, but that's the job.
C: Is there anything you do over and over to stop something bad happening, or to feel less anxious?
P: No. I'm not a worrier. The only stress I've got is arguments, with her and with my lot at work, about how things should be done.
C: Mood? Sleep? Alcohol?
P: Fine. Fine. Couple of beers on a Friday.
C: Any thoughts of harming yourself or anyone else?
P: No.
