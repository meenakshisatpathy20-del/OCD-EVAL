First assessment, Ms L (19), first-year student, self-referred. C = clinician, P = patient.

P: My mum says I'm just a perfectionist, like my dad. Maybe that's all it is.
C: Tell me what's been happening.
P: My notes. When I write something it feels... not right. Not wrong, exactly. I know what I wrote is correct. It's like a feeling that it isn't finished. And it doesn't go until it feels right.
C: What do you make of that feeling?
P: It's ridiculous. It makes no sense. I hate it.
C: How much of the day is it around?
P: On and off. Maybe two hours if you put it together.
C: What do you do with it?
P: I try to ignore it, most of the time. Sometimes I can shrug it off. Sometimes I can't. It's upsetting, but I cope.
C: And when you can't shrug it off?
P: I rewrite the page. Or I reread the sentence. Again and again, until it clicks. Most evenings that's two hours, two and a half.
C: Do you try not to?
P: Yeah, most of the time. About half the time I can leave the page. Half the time I can't.
C: What happens if someone interrupts you?
P: My flatmate did last week. I felt uneasy, irritable, for a while. Then it was okay.
C: How's the course going?
P: I'm failing two modules. My coursework's late or I don't hand it in. And I've stopped going out in the evenings so I can keep up.
C: Is it like this in other parts of your life? Your room, plans, other people?
P: [laughs] My room's a tip. I'm really relaxed about plans. It's just the writing.
C: Mood? Sleep? Alcohol, drugs?
P: A bit low, because of the course. Nothing else.
C: Any thoughts of harming yourself or anyone else?
P: No.
