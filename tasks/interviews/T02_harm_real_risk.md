First assessment, Mr D (34), referred by GP. C = clinician, P = patient.

C: Your GP says you've been struggling with your thoughts.
P: I can't get him out of my head. My old business partner. Eight months ago he walked out and took half our clients and a lot of money with him.
C: What goes through your head?
P: What I'd do to him. I picture it. I play it over and over. Hours a day, some days.
C: Do the thoughts bother you?
P: Bother me? [laughs] He's the one who should be bothered. I know it's not right, thinking like that. My mum would be horrified. But honestly it's the only thing that makes me feel better.
C: Do you try to stop them?
P: Why would I?
C: Have you done anything about it?
P: I found out where he lives now. Drove past a couple of times. Late, so nobody'd see.
C: Anything else?
P: Bought a hunting knife last week.
C: What's that for?
P: [shrugs] You never know.
C: How have you been otherwise? Sleep, drinking?
P: Sleep's rubbish. Five pints most nights, more than I used to.
C: Any experiences like hearing voices, or feeling something strange is going on?
P: No. Nothing like that. I'm not mad.
C: Have you ever been violent before?
P: A fight when I was nineteen. Nothing since.
C: Any thoughts of harming yourself?
P: No.
